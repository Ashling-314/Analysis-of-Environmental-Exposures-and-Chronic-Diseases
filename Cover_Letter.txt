Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Systematic Mendelian Randomization Analysis of Environmental Exposures and Chronic Diseases: Evidence from Univariate MR, Multivariable MR, Mediation MR, and Colocalization" for consideration as an Original Research Article in Environmental Research.

Environmental exposures—tobacco smoking, alcohol consumption, coffee intake, dietary factors, and air pollution (NO₂, PM2.5)—are among the most important environmental agents affecting human health. We conducted a systematic Mendelian randomization (MR) study evaluating 6 environmental exposures across 8 chronic diseases (48 exposure–outcome pairs), using a multi-layered analytical framework:

1. **Univariate MR** identified 14 significant associations, with smoking showing consistent effects on 6 diseases (COPD OR = 1.93, lung cancer OR = 1.59, CAD OR = 1.29, among others).

2. **Multivariable MR** confirmed that smoking's effects are independent of alcohol consumption for CAD, lung cancer, T2D, and heart failure.

3. **Three-step mediation MR** using eQTLGen blood eQTL, validated in GTEx v8 lung and whole blood tissues, identified 10 significant gene expression pathways with tissue-specific eQTL patterns—4 of 6 testable genes were eGenes in lung tissue.

4. **Colocalization analysis** at ±150 kb and ±50 kb windows, combined with comprehensive sensitivity analyses (F-statistics, Steiger directionality, leave-one-out, MR-PRESSO, heterogeneity, MR-Egger intercept), provides a nuanced assessment of the genetic architecture underlying these associations.

We believe this work aligns closely with Environmental Research's scope of investigating the health effects of environmental agents. Our systematic evaluation of multiple environmental exposures—including air pollution, dietary factors, and lifestyle behaviors—across multiple disease outcomes, combined with biological mechanism elucidation through multi-tissue eQTL mediation analysis, represents a comprehensive approach to environmental epidemiology using genetic instruments.

We transparently report the limitations of our findings, including Steiger directionality test results and negative colocalization, and provide a nuanced discussion of their implications for causal inference. All data are publicly available (IEU OpenGWAS, eQTLGen, GTEx v8), and analysis code is deposited at GitHub (https://github.com/Ashling-314/Analysis-of-Environmental-Exposures-and-Chronic-Diseases).

This work has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere. We have no conflicts of interest to declare.
